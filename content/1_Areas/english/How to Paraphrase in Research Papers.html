Introduction

Definition

Paraphrasing is restating someone else's ideas or information in your own words. You must change the sentence structure and vocabulary. It does not use quotation marks, but always requires a citation to credit the original author.

Purposes to Use Paraphrase

  1. save space.
  2. truncate information.
  3. make complex information easier to understand in your study

When to Use Paraphrase or Quote

Paraphrase

  1. To show you understand the main ideas or arguments of the author.
  2. To help explain difficult concepts or terms (in the source text).
  3. To highlight original ideas that are interesting when the original language is not interesting.
  4. To change the emphasis of information to match your arguments.
  5. To provide a clear authorial voice.

Quote

  1. When the original wording is strong and engaging (and concise) on its own.
  2. If the quote is well-known or difficult to paraphrase.
  3. If the words are from an authority and lend direct support to your ideas.
  4. When you want to present the author's detailed methods, findings, or exact position.
  • Both Not Too Often !

How to Paraphase

Steps

  1. Read the source material untill you get it.
  2. Take notes and list key terms of source material.
  3. Write your own paragraph(with out looking at source material).
  4. Check that your version captures the intent and important information.
  5. Provide in-text(parenthetical) citation.

Tips

  1. change the voice when paraphrasing
  2. use a thesaurus but make sure you understand precise meaning and context.

Using Reporting Verbs to Show Your Position.

Neutrality

  • believe, argue, conclude, observe, propose, write

Agreement

  • show, prove, demostrate, provide evidence, confirm, point out

Disagreement

  • neglect, pay little attention to, overlook, fail to note, disregard

Golden Rules

  1. Neve Copy
  2. Always Include a Citation
  3. Focus on Your Study(Maintain the balance)
